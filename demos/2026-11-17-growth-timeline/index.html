Growth timeline detective

Riverbend Supply Co. is a made-up online shop that sells camping and outdoor cookware. A competitor wants to know how its Google traffic grew. This page lines up 24 months of fictional organic visits and brand searches with dated signals, then lists what sat next to each jump.

It is a static demo of the technique: mark months where traffic steps up, and treat nearby signals as candidate causes.

Fictional example data. Every visit count, brand search, headline, price, ad, sitemap total, job post, tool install, and AI citation on this page was written for riverbend.example. The organic line is a camping season laid on a planted base: flat through August 2025, higher from September 2025, higher again from August 2026. December 2025 spikes for one month and gives it back. The March 2026 job post does not move that base. Correlation isn't causation.

Signal types

Turning a type off hides it on the chart and in the candidate lists. The traffic steps stay. A step is a month that beats the prior 3-month mean by at least the sensitivity, and whose next month keeps at least a third of that gain. A one-month spike, such as the fictional December ads, does not count. Nearby qualifying months collapse to the largest jump.

Steps and candidate causes

Each card is a marked step. Candidates are enabled signals inside the window, closest first. Same-month signals are a cluster: closeness does not prove which one did the work.

Correlation isn't causation

One seeded coincidence: a job post for a seasonal warehouse associate is dated March 2026, next to the April 2026 traffic jump. April 2025 jumped by about the same percent, with no job post beside it. April 2026 has more visits because the September step stayed, but the percent jump is the camping season. Seasonality adjust compares this April with last April, using the same 3-month test. The step shrinks. Hiring a warehouse associate for the busy season did not create the camping season.

Brand searches barely move in April. They climb hard in August 2026, when the fictional ads and AI answers name the shop. The August step moves the other way under the seasonal toggle: August 2025 was a fade after summer, so beating last August makes that jump larger.

    Try this on a real competitor (free sources)

    These are text links. This page does not query them. Read each site's terms, and label an estimate as an estimate.

    • Wayback Machine — open their homepage across months and write down headline changes.
    • The site's /sitemap.xml — append /sitemap.xml to their domain, and follow any sitemap index. Count URLs, and note when new sections appear.
    • Meta Ad Library — search the advertiser. Note when an ad started and, if the library shows it, when it stopped.
    • Google Trends — compare a brand query with a category query, monthly, so a seasonal category does not get mistaken for a brand win.
    • ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini — ask a buyer's question, such as who they recommend for camp cookware, and write down which brands and pages they name. Ask more than one.

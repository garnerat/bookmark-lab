Voice note, grill, spec, then build

Interrogate the idea before anything gets built.

That is the step /grill-me is for in the stack Greg describes. The skill itself comes from the mattpocock/skills pattern he names in the post. Here the “voice note” is a chip or a short sentence, and the interrogation is a fixed question bank.

  1. The sentence. One vague idea, the way someone would say it out loud.
  2. The grill. One question at a time. Shallow stops at three. Deep keeps going through six checks, and a risky answer can add a seventh or an eighth.
  3. The spec. One paragraph assembled from the answers. It does not invent a fact you did not pick.
  4. The build. Not on this page. The last panel is the smaller slice the grill would start with, set next to the pile a vague sentence usually drags in.

Answers you gave

    Why the grill comes before the build

    The vague sentence hides a vendor project. A quoting bot becomes a live price engine. A review text becomes an SMS carrier and a compliance program. A booking chatbot becomes a calendar integration. An ads bot becomes an API that can spend. None of those were in the sentence, and all of them show up if you start building from the sentence alone.

    The grill’s job is to make the first week smaller than that sentence, and to leave a paragraph a builder can follow: who it is for, what it may not do, how you will know it worked, and who is on the hook when it is wrong.

    Try it yourself

    1. Write the idea in one sentence, the way you would say it out loud.
    2. Run the deep questions with the person who does the job, not only the person who wants the bot.
    3. Keep the first build inside the slice. If you could not do that slice by hand for a week, it is still too big.
    4. Write the real spec after that. Open a vendor account only when the slice needs it.

    The choices on this page will not match your shop. They are here so the shape of a grill is easy to see.

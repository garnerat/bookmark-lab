LLM-to-code harness

Saltbox Kitchen is a fictional restaurant with two kitchens, East Market and River Ward. One back office pays the vendors: produce, meat, dairy, linens, and beer and wine. The same invoices show up every week.

Andrew bookmarked @_aj:

We built a new harness using @typesafeai's Jev that cuts the cost of repetitive work by 90%. The harness learns the job as it runs, moving steps from LLM calls to code. Running 100,000 compliance alerts costs >$290K on Opus 5. With agentrun() we got it down to <$26K.

A follow-up says the workflow DSL is open source at agentrun.ai. The long version is Miguel Rios Berrios, “AgentRun: a harness for repetitive knowledge work” (18 Sep 2026). An agent does the repetitive job, then more of it becomes a workflow of code, typed questions, and a model only on the odd cases. On the authors' figures a full Opus 5 agent was about $2.89 a compliance alert and the tuned workflow about $0.25, and 100,000 alerts about $290K versus about $26K. Jev, from TypeSafe, is described there as a typed question answered in about 150 milliseconds. The site describes the project as open source under Apache-2.0. This page does not run Jev, the DSL, or any model.

Illustrative simulation. Saltbox Kitchen, the two kitchens, and the vendors were invented for this page. A model step is priced here at $0.03 and 1.6 seconds. A replayed step is $0.00 and 8 milliseconds. Those rates are round numbers, not a quote from Opus, Jev, or anyone else. Illustrative numbers, not benchmarks. Nothing on this page calls a network.

What this sketch is doing

What it is. Each invoice takes five steps: name the vendor, read the lines, file them on the chart of accounts, flag any price more than 8% above the last order, and send it to the bookkeeper or the manager. Every step starts as a model call.

Why the bill falls. River & Rind, Hale & Brisket, Millhouse, Linen & Loom, and Cork & Cask keep sending the same kind of invoice. After enough identical answers, the harness saves that answer and replays it. A replay is free in this sketch. The all-model line keeps climbing. The harness line flattens. A handwritten slip never matches a saved answer, so it stays on the model. That is the odd case the write-up keeps a fallback for.

When it is dangerous. A saved answer is not the 8% rule. “Hale & Brisket is in band” does not compare this invoice with the last one. When chuck goes up 14%, or River & Rind moves its columns, or chef coats show up on a linen bill, the replay still says last month's answer and looks finished. A spot-check sends every Nth invoice back through the model for the steps that already graduated. A disagreement deletes that rule. The check costs money. Skipping it is cheaper until the invoice changes.

The write-up's point is to graduate the procedure: code does the arithmetic, a typed question does a closed decision, and the model is reserved for what still has to be read. This sketch graduates by replaying a repeated answer. That is the cheap version, and it is the version that misses a jump.

Try it

Three runs, same 320 invoices. Each button sets the controls and starts the stream.

  1. Watch the five steps flip from LLM to CODE. The harness cost peels away from the all-model line.
  2. The surprise hits at invoice 144. With no spot-check, the catch rate on jumps over 8% falls to 0%, and chef coats are filed to Linens.
  3. Every 10th invoice is asked of the model again. A disagreement knocks that rule back to LLM, and the catch rate recovers.

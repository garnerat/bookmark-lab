Most of the bot bill is re-reading

Mill Creek Plumbing is a made-up shop in Northside, Cincinnati. Two techs, a dispatcher, and the owner. Give it a bot team, run a month, and see how much of the token bill is old chat being read again.

The ladder is from a workshop by Lauren Tan, a SpaceXAI engineer, as quoted by @0xg4tsby: set the codebase up so that even a weak agent can do the job. The related write-up is Grok Bot: 6 Bots, 200+ Agents, a Smaller Bill by @m4lkxx. In that export, about 75% of tokens went to re-reading old chat, and about 1% went to the work.

Mill Creek Plumbing is fictional. The token counts, the prices, and the mistake rates are illustrative example data, not measurements and not a vendor quote. Nothing is uploaded. The page does not call a model or a network.

Climb the ladder before you add a bot

You spend the tokens to set up a codebase so that even the dumbest agents can do a good job.

Lauren Tan, quoted in the workshop post. The order there is Job, then Rules, then Skills, then Verification, and only then more bots.

  1. Job. The plumbing work. On this page that is the “new work” tokens.
  2. Rules and skills. Written once, into a short file, then read on each run. Pasting the same instructions into every bot charges them again on every wake.
  3. Verification. One check for the team, also paid once. It lowers the chance a run drops a detail. It does not need another bot per task.
  4. Bots. A few domain owners, plus one ops bot. A bot per task multiplies handoffs, and each handoff is another chance to drop a detail.

Where mistakes come from

Why this matters

The workshop’s point is the order. Job, rules, skills, and a check come before a bigger team. People add another bot when the first one fails. That multiplies the mistake, and it multiplies the chat that every new bot re-reads.

The write-up’s export is the bill side of the same idea. About 75% of the tokens were old chat, and about 1% were the work. Rules and checks are paid once. Re-reading is paid on every run. The 6-bot setup in that post, and the 200+ agents around it, are a larger team than this shop. The shape of the leak is the same.

Try it yourself

  1. Export one bot’s usage for a week.
  2. Split the tokens into re-reading old chat, reading rules or skills, and new work.
  3. Fix one leak. Compact the history, slow a routine that wakes too often, merge two bots that share a job, or move repeated instructions into a short rules file.
  4. Compare the same split next week.

The numbers on this page will not match an export. They are here so the shape of the leak is easy to see.

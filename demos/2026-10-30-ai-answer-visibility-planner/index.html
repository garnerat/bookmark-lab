Linwood Comfort is a made-up residential HVAC shop on the east side of Cincinnati. Each row is a question a homeowner asks before hiring, written once as a Google keyword and once as an AI prompt. You diagnose why the shop is missing, apply one fix, and rerun the prompts. Google rank and AI mentions are scored apart.

The loop is from this post on how Granola grew new users from AI search. The post says monthly new users from AI search rose 4,600% in seven months. This page does not reproduce that. The shop, the ranks, the listings, and the thread are a sample of the loop.

All names, ranks, listings, and threads are fictional sample data. Linwood Comfort is not a real company. Queen City Trades is not a real directory. Nothing is uploaded. The page does not call a model or a network. The “AI” column is a seeded sample of 20 reruns.

The loop, in plain words

  1. Start from the decision a buyer is making, not from a keyword list. Turn that decision into a Google keyword and an AI prompt.
  2. Before writing anything, say why a lost prompt lost. Four causes: no page, a page that does not answer, a crawl or index block, or an answer coming from someone else.
  3. If a page exists, improve it. Publish a new page only when nothing on the site answers the question.
  4. Fix a technical issue only when it sits on a page tied to one of these questions. A heavy homepage is not that.
  5. Read the sources the simulated answer already cited.
  6. Sort each one. OWN pages you control. CLAIM profiles you can take over. EARN reviews, editorial, and community mentions. CONTEST outdated or wrong information.
  7. Use the same buyer questions to pick a community thread. One thread, not every forum.
  8. Track Google rank and AI mentions as two scores. They will not move together.
  9. Make one change, rerun the prompts, and repeat the kind of change that moved the score you care about.

Split the reading from the planning

A neighborhood cleaning shop asks which customers have not booked in 90 days. Most of the tokens are the files. Run that task on one frontier model, or hand the files to a cheap reader and let the frontier model see a short summary.

The shape is from Yarchi’s post on Shopify’s agent setup: the expensive model talks to the user and plans, reading-heavy jobs go to small models, and the expensive model only gets the condensed result. The 7× speedup and the roughly 90% token cut are Shopify’s reported numbers, via that post. This page does not measure them.

Fictional shop, example prices, no model call. Wren & Co. and the customers are made up. Token counts are round estimates. The prices and speeds below are knobs for this page, not a quote from Shopify or any model vendor. Nothing is uploaded.

A judge on the summary

The cheap read is safe when the note still has what the plan named. This check looks for five fields. It is a stand-in, on one made-up summary.

The source post describes Shopify’s separate judge, tuned until it agreed with human experts: 37.7, then 74.5, slightly above a frontier model’s 73. Failed conversations are rerun at night and the fixes become training data; 69% of the failures it tried were repaired. Those are their reported figures, via the post. Copying the split on a coding agent can be as small as a cheap subagent that reads files. This page does not train anything.

Cash Applications Bot sandbox

Greenline Commercial Landscaping is a made-up company that bills property managers, HOAs, and office parks for grounds work. This page runs one morning payment feed the way a cash-applications bot would: post an exact match, write off a small bank fee, read remittance advice when one payment covers several invoices, and stop when a sender’s domain is not an exact match.

Andrew bookmarked @bot, 18 Nov 2026, and the write-up Building an accounting team with Grok Bot:

The Bot checks our payment feed twice daily and automatically applies payments that match outstanding invoices exactly. When a payment doesn’t match, it searches for remittance advice that can help identify which invoices the payment covers. This process ordinarily takes an accountant three to four hours. The Bot completes it in roughly 30 minutes.

The write-up gives each bot one job: Provisioning, Billing, Billing Support, Cash Applications, and Collections. A bot reads an operating manual it cannot edit. Low-risk work runs on its own. Credits, refunds, and write-offs above a limit wait for a designated person. A customer-facing check treats a similar-looking email domain as a reason to escalate. Onboarding is about three weeks: watch, then propose, then do the routine work.

Fictional books, plain rules. Every customer, invoice, payment, and email was written for this page. Nothing here is a real business or a real person. The rules are ordinary JavaScript in the browser. There is no model call, no network request, and no connection to a bank.

    Try this in your business

    • Write the operating manual first. Put the write-off cap, the approval limit, and the approved domains in a doc the bot can read and cannot edit.
    • Start in propose mode. Let it explain each payment, and approve the routine ones for a week before it posts on its own.
    • Keep refunds, credits, and write-offs above your limit behind a designated person. Let exact matches go through.
